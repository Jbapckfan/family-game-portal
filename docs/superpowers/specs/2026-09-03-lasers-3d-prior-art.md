# Research Report: Top-Down Laser Puzzle with Hidden Heights and Climbing Mirrors

## 1. Does it already exist?

**Verdict: No.** The exact combination was not found anywhere: a top-down 2D-looking laser and mirror puzzle whose camera tilt reveals hidden wall heights, plus a compound-angle mirror that deflects the beam 90 degrees sideways and starts a 45-degree climb (one level per cell) that persists through flat mirrors. Every candidate that scored above 40 in the first pass was refuted on verification; corrected similarities top out at 44. The pieces exist separately in the wild (3D laser puzzles with view rotation, multi-floor beam routing via up/down mirrors, perspective-reveal walking puzzles, flat mirror-inventory grids), but no product combines the disguised-3D reveal, the climbing wedge mirror, a placed-mirror inventory with par, or a no-tilt star. The "perspective reveal" half lives in non-laser games (A Little Perspective, Arthisio, Perspective, Crush); the "beam changes elevation" half lives in openly 3D games (RuneScape Temple of Light, Khet 3D, Laseredstone, Circuit: Laser Maze) where nothing is hidden and vertical travel is a pure 90-degree axis swap, never a diagonal climb across cells.

Notable unverified items that bracket the concept from either side: ThinkFun Laser Maze (2013) is exactly the concept's 2D surface layer (5x5 grid, limited mirror inventory, challenge cards) with zero height; Laseredstone (Minecraft mod, 2025) has a voxel vertical-to-horizontal mirror block but axis-aligned beams only; A Little Perspective (2025) and Arthisio (2025) do the rotate-to-reveal trick with no lasers.

## 2. Closest neighbours

- Laser Quest! (Infinity Games): closest in modality and audience, 3D laser puzzle with rotate-to-reveal on mobile and web. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/laser-quest/id1520222059
- Circuit: Laser Maze: closest mechanical skeleton, placed redirectors on a true 3D grid with vertical mazes. https://store.steampowered.com/app/1870710/Circuit_Laser_Maze/
- ThinkFun Laser Maze: the concept's flat layer exactly (grid, emitter, targets, limited 45-degree mirror inventory, single-solution challenges). https://www.ravensburger.us/en-US/products/games/thinkfun/laser-maze-44001014
- Laseredstone (Minecraft mod): voxel grid with a mirror block that swaps a beam between vertical and horizontal travel, so beams pass over or under blocks by height. https://modrinth.com/mod/laseredstone
- A Little Perspective: the rotate-the-camera-to-change-what-geometry-is-real half of the idea, grid-based and kid-readable, without lasers. https://store.steampowered.com/app/3485300

## 3. Ways to make it more unique

Ranked by judge score. Rainbow Altitude Beam (29.67) is a near-duplicate of Altitude Is a Color and is folded into that entry per the judges; Crate Inventory takes the eighth slot.

1. **Skipping-Stone Floor Mirrors** (score 31.7, build: medium). Puddle tiles are mirrors lying flat in the floor: a level beam glides over untouched, but a descending beam bounces back up at 45 degrees keeping its heading. A dip mirror followed by spaced puddles turns the beam into a skipping stone whose peaks must land over walls and whose troughs must miss low obstacles. From above the fair tell is a periodic bright dot where beam and shadow coincide. Judge note: hop spacing (wavelength) is a genuinely new puzzle verb and vertical-only reflection is a clean rule; no prior art has horizontal floor mirrors, but it needs the dip mirror to exist first and careful rules for descending onto walls.

2. **Tide Dial** (30.3, medium). A water slider floods the level from the lowest cells up, painting every submerged cell blue, so dragging it is a contour map the kid builds themselves. Water also swallows any beam at or below the waterline and drowns placed mirrors, and par assumes a specific tide. Counts as a partial reveal, so a strict star can forbid moving it. Judge note: a translucent plane at height h plus per-cell tint is easy to build; the real cost is balancing par at a fixed tide.

3. **Stilt Mirrors** (30.0, medium). Any inventory mirror can be dropped on top of a wall segment, where it sits at that wall's height and only catches beams at that level. From above it looks identical to a floor mirror; tilt shows it perched on a pillar, and a runaway climbing beam snaps into it mid-air. Judge note: placement gaining a z coordinate from hidden terrain is exactly the game's thesis and is only a height offset plus a per-height mirror check; Abiotic Factor stacks reflectors for beam height but never on a disguised grid.

4. **Altitude Is a Color** (30.0, small; merged with Rainbow Altitude Beam, 29.7). The beam's hue encodes its height: red on the floor, amber one up, yellow two, white at three. Targets are tinted, so "reach the target" becomes "arrive at the right floor", including a sky lantern on flat ground that wants white light. A late prism splits a beam into floor and one-up components. Judge note: the cheapest high-impact no-tilt tell in the list (per-segment material color keyed to z), and "my laser turned green" lands with a six-year-old; the trade-off is that color partially gives away the hidden terrain.

5. **Architect vs Solver** (30.0, medium). Hot-seat on one phone: the Architect uses the tilted view to raise walls, move the target onto a plateau, and swap in a sloped mirror; the game verifies solvability and computes par, then the camera flattens on handoff and the Solver gets limited tilts. Architect scores per tilt spent, Solver scores for solving without one. Judge note: the best social hook here and unlike anything in prior art (Khet is competitive but symmetric); the live solvability BFS over cells x orientations x mirrors is combinatorial and needs pruning.

6. **Arches and Windows** (29.7, small). Two wall types pixel-identical to a wall from above: an arch blocks only elevated beams (floor beams go under), a window is solid at floor level and open at exactly one altitude. The same wall grid now holds three stacked maze topologies. Tells: arches show a light gap in their shadow, windows show a bright square of floor through the opening. Judge note: best depth-per-effort in the list, a pure per-cell height-mask check in the existing tracer, but invisible-from-above rules can frustrate, so tells must be loud.

7. **Shade Targets** (29.7, medium). Moss patches, night flowers, and sleeping cats must not be lit; they wake only when the beam's shadow falls on them. A beam climbing over a wall casts a dark stripe the player steers by steering the light, so puzzles ask for a lantern hit and a shadow hit from one beam. Judge note: the friendlier skin of Shadow Target (pick one), repurposing the tell the game already draws as a goal; the rule for a shadow landing on a plateau top needs a clean definition.

8. **Crate Inventory** (29.3, medium). Alongside mirrors the inventory holds wooden crates that raise any floor cell one level; a mirror on a crate reflects beams one story up, and a crate is a wall to a ground beam. Since the player placed them, crates are the only terrain they know for certain, so puzzles become building known geometry against unknown geometry. A crate dropped on a hidden plateau stacks to two and casts a longer shadow, a sneaky fair tell. Judge note: editing the height map with a budget is the deepest lever here; prior art has obstacle pushing and reflector stacking, but never as an inventory against hidden terrain.
